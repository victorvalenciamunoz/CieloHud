# Zorra

Una zorrita con un ganso en la boca, que Hevelius puso en 1687 junto a otros cazadores del cielo: el Águila y la Lira, que
entonces también se veía como un buitre. Decía que llevaba el ganso a Cerbero, otra figura suya, para desayunar juntos;
Cerbero desapareció de los mapas, y el ganso también. En ella está la Percha, una barra de estrellas con un gancho, que
al-Sufi ya describió en el año 964.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Vulpecula (ianridpath.com/startales/vulpecula.html): «A constellation introduced by the Polish astronomer Johannes Hevelius in his star catalogue of 1687 [...] he depicted it as a double figure of a fox, Vulpecula, carrying in its jaws a goose, Anser. Since then the goose has flown (or been eaten), leaving just the fox»; «Hevelius placed the fox near two other hunting animals, the eagle (the constellation Aquila) and the vulture (which was an alternative identification for Lyra)»; «the fox was taking the goose to neighbouring Cerberus, another of his inventions – although this part of the tableau has been spoiled, as Cerberus is now obsolete»; «He explained that the fox was taking the goose to Cerberus for a shared breakfast»; «an asterism known as Brocchi's Cluster, or more popularly the Coathanger because of its distinctive bar-and-hook shape [...] it was first mentioned by the Arab astronomer al-Ṣūfī in his Book of the Fixed Stars, written in AD 964».
