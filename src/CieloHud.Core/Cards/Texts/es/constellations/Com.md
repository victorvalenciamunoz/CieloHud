# Cabellera de Berenice

La melena de Berenice, una reina de Egipto que existió: prometió cortársela si su marido, Ptolomeo III, volvía victorioso
de la guerra. Cumplió y la dejó en un templo, pero al día siguiente había desaparecido, y el astrónomo Conón dijo que se
había ido al cielo; seguramente, un montaje para glorificar a los reyes. En ella hay un cúmulo de estrellas que el
astrónomo Ptolomeo llamaba «el rizo».

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Coma Berenices (ianridpath.com/startales/comaberenices.html): «Berenice was a real person who, in 246 BC, married her cousin, Ptolemy III Euergetes»; «Ptolemy set out to attack Asia on the Third Syrian War. Berenice vowed that if he returned victorious she would cut off her hair in gratitude to the gods»; «she [...] placed her hair in the temple [...] But the following day the tresses were missing»; «Conon of Samos [...] pointed out the group of stars near the tail of the lion, telling the king that the hair of Berenice had gone to join the constellations»; «In reality, the disappearance of the hair and its subsequent 'discovery' among the stars was most likely staged to glorify Ptolemy and his queen among their subjects»; Ptolomeo lo describió como «'a nebulous mass, called the lock'»; «the nebulous mass described by Ptolemy [...] is now known as the cluster Melotte 111».
