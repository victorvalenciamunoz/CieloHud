# Orión

Para los sumerios era su héroe Gilgamesh luchando contra el Toro del Cielo, que es Tauro; para Homero, un gigante
cazador, y para los chinos, un guerrero. Se reconoce por el cinturón, tres estrellas en fila entre otras cuatro
brillantes. Del cinturón cuelga la espada, con una mancha borrosa que se ve a simple vista en noches despejadas: la
nebulosa de Orión, donde están naciendo estrellas.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Orion (ianridpath.com/startales/orion.html): «the constellation originated with the Sumerians, who saw in it their great hero Gilgamesh fighting the Bull of Heaven»; «Taurus was GUD AN-NA, bull of heaven»; «Homer in the Odyssey describes Orion as a giant hunter»; «a distinctive line of three stars forming his belt».
- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Orion 2 (ianridpath.com/startales/orion2.html): «Chinese astronomers knew Orion as Shen, a great hunter or warrior, one of the rare cases in which a constellation was visualized almost exactly the same way in China as in Europe»; «the four that make up the traditional outline of Orion (Alpha, Gamma, Beta, and Kappa), the three stars of the belt, and three stars in the sword»; «Below the belt lies a hazy patch marking the giant's sword or hunting knife. This is the location of the Orion Nebula [...] a mass of gas from which an immense swarm of stars is being born»; «it is visible to the naked eye on clear nights».
