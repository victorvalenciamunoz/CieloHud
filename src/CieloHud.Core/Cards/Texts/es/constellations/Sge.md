# Flecha

Una flecha suelta: Arato decía que está sola, sin arco. Hay al menos tres historias de quién la disparó: Apolo, contra los
cíclopes que forjaron el rayo que mató a su hijo Asclepio; Hércules, contra el águila que cada día comía el hígado de
Prometeo; o Eros, para enamorar a Zeus de Ganímedes. En esta última, la guarda el Águila, que está a su lado.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Sagitta (ianridpath.com/startales/sagitta.html): «Aratus described it as 'alone, without a bow' since there is no sign of the archer who might have shot it»; «There are at least three different stories to account for the arrow in the sky and its shooter. Eratosthenes said it was the projectile with which Apollo killed the Cyclopes because they made the thunderbolts of Zeus that struck down Apollo's son, Asclepius»; «Hyginus, on the other hand, said that Sagitta was one of the arrows with which Heracles killed the eagle that ate the liver of Prometheus»; «Germanicus Caesar identified Sagitta as the arrow of Eros which kindled in Zeus his passion for the shepherd boy Ganymede [...] Now, according to Germanicus, the arrow is guarded in the sky by the eagle of Zeus – and Sagitta does indeed lie next to the constellation of the eagle, Aquila».
