# Unicornio

Un unicornio que los griegos no conocían: lo puso en el cielo el holandés Petrus Plancius en 1612, en el mismo globo que
la Jirafa, en un hueco entre la Hidra y Orión donde no había figura griega. Es moderno, sin leyendas, y ninguna de sus
estrellas tiene nombre. Está en la Vía Láctea y contiene la nebulosa Roseta, una corona de gas brillante con estrellas
dentro.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Monoceros (ianridpath.com/startales/monoceros.html): «The mythical single-horned beast, the unicorn, is represented by this constellation which was unknown to the ancient Greeks. Monoceros was introduced in 1612 [...] on a globe by the Dutch theologian and cartographer Petrus Plancius. This was the same globe on which Camelopardalis, another of his inventions, first appeared»; «Monoceros fills a large area between Hydra and Orion where there was no Greek constellation»; «it lies in the Milky Way and contains a host of fascinating objects, most notably the Rosette Nebula, a wreath-shaped mass of glowing gas with embedded stars»; «There are no legends associated with the constellation, as it is a modern figure, and none of its stars has a name».
