# Lince

Un lince que el astrónomo polaco Johannes Hevelius puso en 1687 en un hueco entre la Osa Mayor y Auriga. Escribió que para
verlo haría falta tener ojos de lince, aunque exageró lo débiles que son sus estrellas. Él mismo tenía fama de vista de
lince: siguió midiendo las estrellas a simple vista mucho después de que los demás usaran miras telescópicas.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Lynx (ianridpath.com/startales/lynx.html): «Johannes Hevelius, the Polish astronomer who introduced this constellation in his star catalogue of 1687, continued to measure star positions with the naked eye long after other astronomers had adopted telescopic sights. The French astronomer Pierre Gassendi (1592–1655) wrote in 1644 that Hevelius had the 'eyes of a lynx'»; «Hevelius wrote in the Introduction to his catalogue that anyone who wanted to observe it would need the eyesight of a lynx ('oculos habeat Lynceos'), although he undoubtedly exaggerated the faintness of the 19 stars he catalogued in it»; «Lynx fills a blank area of sky between Ursa Major and Auriga».
