# Corona Austral

Para los griegos no era una corona, sino una guirnalda: Arato hablaba de un círculo de estrellas a los pies de Sagitario,
e Higino decía que el arquero la había tirado como jugando. Quizá sea la corona que Dioniso puso en el cielo tras rescatar
a su madre del inframundo; Higino lo cuenta en la Corona Boreal, pero puede que confundiera las dos.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Corona Australis (ianridpath.com/startales/coronaaustralis.html): «Corona Australis was known to the Greeks not as a crown but as a wreath»; «Aratus did not name it as a separate constellation but referred to it simply as a circlet of stars beneath the forefeet of Sagittarius. Hyginus said it was a wreath cast off by the archer 'as by one at play'»; «there seem to be no legends associated with it, unless this is the crown placed in the sky by Dionysus after retrieving his dead mother from the Underworld. Hyginus gives this myth under the Northern Crown (Corona Borealis) but it seems out of place there and he may have confused the two constellations».
