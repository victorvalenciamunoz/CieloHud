# Osa Menor

Según los griegos, la dio a conocer el sabio Tales de Mileto, hace más de 2500 años. Los fenicios navegaban guiándose
por ella y no por la Osa Mayor: es más pequeña y débil, pero está más cerca del polo. Sus siete estrellas forman el
Carro Menor, con la Estrella Polar en la punta de la cola. Las dos Osas están espalda con espalda, con la cola del Dragón
entre ellas.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Ursa Minor (ianridpath.com/startales/ursaminor.html): «The Little Bear was said by the Greeks to have been first named by the astronomer Thales of Miletus, who lived from about 625 to 545 BC»; «the Phoenicians navigated by reference to Ursa Minor rather than Ursa Major»; «although the Little Bear is smaller and fainter than the Great Bear, it lies closer to the pole and hence provides a better guide to true north»; «These seven stars have a similar ladle shape to the Big Dipper of Ursa Major, and so are popularly termed the Little Dipper. At the end of the Little Bear's tail (or the dipper's handle) is the star Alpha Ursae Minoris, commonly known by the Latin name Polaris»; «In the sky the two bears stand back to back, facing in opposite directions, with the tail of Draco the dragon between them».
