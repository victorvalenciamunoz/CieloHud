# Tauro

Zeus convertido en un toro blanco para raptar a Europa, a la que llevó a nado hasta Creta. En el cielo solo está la mitad
delantera del toro: la cabeza es una V de estrellas, con dos cuernos largos. Cerca de la punta de uno de ellos estalló en
1054 una supernova que los astrónomos chinos vieron de día durante semanas; sus restos son la nebulosa del Cangrejo, que
solo se ve con telescopio.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Taurus (ianridpath.com/startales/taurus.html): «it was said to represent Zeus in the disguise he adopted for another of his extramarital affairs, this time as the bull that carried away Europa»; «His hide was white as fresh snow»; «it began to swim strongly out to sea»; «Eventually, the bull waded ashore at Crete»; «with star-tipped horns and a head defined by a V-shaped group of stars»; «In the sky, only the front half of the bull is shown».
- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Taurus 2 (ianridpath.com/startales/taurus2.html): «Near the tip of the bull's right horn, which is marked by Zeta Tauri, lies the remarkable Crab Nebula»; «a stellar explosion, seen from Earth in AD 1054, that was bright enough to be visible in daylight for three weeks»; «the Crab Nebula is the shattered remnant of the star that blew up, now visible only through telescopes».
- NASA, Hubble, Messier 1 (science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/messier-1/): «In 1054, Chinese astronomers took notice of a "guest star" that was, for nearly a month, visible in the daytime sky»; «the Crab Nebula can be spotted with a small telescope». Ridpath dice tres semanas y la NASA casi un mes: el texto dice «durante semanas».
