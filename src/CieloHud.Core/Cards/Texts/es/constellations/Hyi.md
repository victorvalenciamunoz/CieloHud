# Hidra Macho

Las serpientes de mar que los navegantes holandeses Keyser y de Houtman vieron en sus viajes, convertidas en la pareja
macho de la Hidra. Es la constelación que más veces se ha rediseñado: Lacaille la hizo pasar entre las dos Nubes de
Magallanes, le cortó la cola para hacer sitio al Octante y le quitó estrellas para el Tucán, el Reloj y el Retículo.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Hydrus (ianridpath.com/startales/hydrus.html): «Hydrus was one of the 12 southern constellations introduced at the end of the 16th century by the Dutch navigators Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman [...] It represents the sea snakes that the Dutch explorers would have seen on their voyages. Hydrus is a male water snake, whereas the much larger Greek constellation Hydra is a female»; «Hydrus has endured more redesigns than any other constellation»; «He rerouted Hydrus to pass between the two Magellanic Clouds, transferring some of its stars to Tucana in the process [...] In addition, Lacaille docked the snake's tail to make way for Octans [...] He also commandeered a couple of stars from Hydrus for Horologium and Reticulum».
