# Perseo

El héroe que cortó la cabeza de Medusa mirándola solo en el reflejo de su escudo, porque la mirada de las gorgonas
convertía en piedra. En la mano de la espada tiene una mancha borrosa que Ptolomeo ya anotó: el Cúmulo Doble, dos
cúmulos de estrellas juntos. Y de Perseo parecen venir las Perseidas, la lluvia de estrellas de mediados de agosto, que se
ve mejor desde el hemisferio norte.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Perseus (ianridpath.com/startales/perseus.html): «Their evil gaze turned to stone anyone who set eyes on them»; «Looking only at her reflection in his brightly polished shield, Perseus swung his diamond sword and decapitated Medusa with one blow»; «The right hand of Perseus, in which he holds his sword, is marked by a feature that Ptolemy in the Almagest termed a 'nebulous mass'. This 'nebulous mass' is, in fact, a twin cluster of stars now popularly called the Double Cluster».
- NASA, Perseids (science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/perseids/): «The Perseids meteor shower peaks in mid-August»; «Their radiant – the point in the sky from which the Perseids appear to come – is the constellation Perseus»; «The Perseids are best viewed in the Northern Hemisphere».
