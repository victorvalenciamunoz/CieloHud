# Lobo

Para los griegos era solo una fiera sin nombre, ensartada en la pica que sostiene Centauro, que la ofrece en sacrificio en
el Altar. Que sea un lobo se fijó en el Renacimiento, y recuerda su origen en Babilonia, donde la llamaban «perro salvaje»
o «lobo»; por eso, en 2024 la IAU dio a su estrella Alfa el nombre de Uridim. Los griegos no tenían mitos para ella, quizá
por ser importada.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Lupus (ianridpath.com/startales/lupus.html): «The ancient Greeks called this constellation Θηρίον (Therion), representing an unspecified wild animal»; «It was visualized as impaled on a long pole called a thyrsus, held by the adjoining constellation of Centaurus»; «Eratosthenes said that the centaur was holding the animal towards the altar (the constellation Ara) as though about to sacrifice it»; «The identification of this constellation with a wolf seems to have become established in Renaissance times, harking back to its Babylonian identification as UR.IDIM, meaning 'wild dog' or 'wolf'»; «The fact that it is an imported constellation probably explains why the Greeks had no myths for it»; «Alpha Lupi was officially named Uridim by the IAU in 2024, in recognition of the constellation's Babylonian origin».
