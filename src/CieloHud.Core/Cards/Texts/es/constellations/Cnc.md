# Cáncer

El cangrejo que mordió a Hércules en un pie mientras luchaba con la Hidra; Hércules lo aplastó, y Hera, su enemiga, lo
puso en el cielo. En su centro hay una mancha borrosa, el Pesebre o la Colmena, con dos estrellas a los lados, los Asnos,
que parecen comer de él. Ptolomeo no sabía qué era: en 1609, Galileo vio con su telescopio que es un cúmulo de estrellas.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Cancer (ianridpath.com/startales/cancer.html): «While Heracles was fighting the multi-headed monster called the Hydra in the swamp near Lerna, the crab emerged from the swamp and added its own attack by biting Heracles on the foot. Heracles angrily stamped on the crab, crushing it [...] the goddess Hera, the enemy of Heracles, put the crab among the stars»; «Gamma and Delta Cancri [...] were known jointly to the Greeks as Ὄνοι (Onoi), the asses»; «Dionysus put the asses in the sky, either side of a hazy-looking patch which the Greeks called Φάτνη (Phatne), the Manger, from which the asses seem to be feeding»; «Ptolemy in the Almagest described Phatne as 'the nebulous mass in the chest' without knowing what it actually was. Its true nature as a cluster of faint stars was not established until 1609 when Galileo first turned a telescope on it»; «Praesepe, but it is popularly termed the Beehive».
