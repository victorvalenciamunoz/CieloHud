# Delfín

El delfín que salvó a Arión, un poeta y músico real del siglo VII a. C.: unos marineros iban a matarlo para robarle, él
pidió cantar una última canción, saltó al mar y un delfín lo llevó a Grecia. Dos de sus estrellas tienen nombres raros,
Sualocin y Rotanev: al revés dicen Nicolaus Venator, el nombre latino de Niccolò Cacciatore, ayudante de Giuseppe Piazzi,
que los publicó en 1814.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Delphinus (ianridpath.com/startales/delphinus.html): «this is the dolphin that saved the life of Arion, a real-life poet and musician of the seventh century BC»; «the sailors plotted to kill him and steal the small fortune that he had earned»; «Arion asked to be allowed to sing one last song»; «Arion leaped overboard – and one of the dolphins carried him on its back to Greece»; «Two stars in Delphinus bear the peculiar names Sualocin and Rotanev, which first appeared in the Palermo Catalogue of 1814 compiled by the Italian astronomer Giuseppe Piazzi (1746–1826). Read backwards, these names spell out Nicolaus Venator, the Latinized form of Niccolò Cacciatore (1770–1841), who was Piazzi's assistant».
