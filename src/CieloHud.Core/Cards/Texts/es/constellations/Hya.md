# Hidra

La serpiente de agua de nueve cabezas que mató Hércules en su segundo trabajo: cada vez que le cortaba una, le salían dos,
hasta que su cochero Yolao empezó a quemar los cuellos cortados. En el cielo solo tiene una cabeza; quizá la inmortal. Es
hembra, aunque Ptolomeo la nombrara en masculino; el macho es la Hidra Macho, una constelación del sur de finales del
siglo XVI.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Hydra (ianridpath.com/startales/hydra.html): «the Hydra was the creature that Heracles fought and killed as the second of his famous labours»; «Hydra reputedly had nine heads, the middle one of which was immortal. In the sky, though, it is shown with one head only – perhaps this is the immortal one»; «no sooner had one head been destroyed than two grew in its place»; «Heracles called for help to his charioteer Iolaus who burned the stump of each head as soon as it was struck off»; «Eratosthenes and Ptolemy both called the constellation Ὕδρος (Hydros), which is the male form of the name; but this water snake is actually female»; «There is now also a male water snake in the sky, the small southern constellation Hydrus, introduced by Dutch navigators at the end of the 16th century».
