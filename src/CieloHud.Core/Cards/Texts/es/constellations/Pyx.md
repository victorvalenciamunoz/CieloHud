# Brújula

Una brújula de navegante, otra invención de Lacaille. Está junto a la popa del Argo, donde iba su mástil, y con las
estrellas que Ptolomeo ponía en él, pero no es una parte del barco, aunque a veces se diga. En 1844, John Herschel propuso
cambiarla por el mástil, sin éxito. Los griegos no podían tenerla en su cielo: no conocían la brújula.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Pyxis (ianridpath.com/startales/pyxis.html): «A small southern constellation invented by the Frenchman Nicolas Louis de Lacaille during his survey of the southern skies in 1751–52. Pyxis represents a magnetic compass as used by seamen. It is located near the stern of the ship Argo in the same area as the ship's mast»; «The four stars that Lacaille labelled Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta Pyxidis had been catalogued by Ptolemy as lying in the mast of Argo»; «It is sometimes asserted that Pyxis is one of the parts into which Lacaille divided the former Argo Navis, but that is not the case»; «there are no legends associated with it – indeed, the magnetic compass was completely unknown to the ancient Greeks»; «In 1844 the English astronomer John Herschel proposed returning Ptolemy's four stars to Argo by replacing Pyxis with a fourth subdivision of the ship which he called Malus, the mast [...] but otherwise Malus was not widely adopted».
