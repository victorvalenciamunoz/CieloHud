# Piscis

Dos peces atados por la cola con una cuerda, idea que los griegos tomaron de Babilonia. Afrodita y su hijo Eros,
huyendo del monstruo Tifón, se echaron al Éufrates y dos peces los salvaron. Uno de los peces es un anillo de siete
estrellas al sur del Gran Cuadrado de Pegaso. En Piscis está el punto donde el Sol cruza el ecuador del cielo en marzo,
que en tiempos de los griegos estaba en Aries.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Pisces (ianridpath.com/startales/pisces.html): «The tails of the two fish are joined by a cord or ribbon marked out by a snaking chain of stars»; «according to the historian Paul Kunitzsch the Babylonians visualized a pair of fish joined by a cord in this area, so evidently the Greeks borrowed this idea»; «The goddess Aphrodite and her son Eros took cover among the reeds on the banks of the Euphrates [...] she called for help to the water nymphs and leapt into the river. In one version of the story, two fish swam up and carried Aphrodite and Eros to safety on their backs»; Gaia «sent this fearsome monster [Typhon] to attack the gods»; «The body of the westward-swimming fish is easy enough to pick out, containing a loop of seven stars called the Circlet south of the Square of Pegasus»; «it contains the point at which the Sun crosses the celestial equator into the northern hemisphere on March 20 each year. This point, called the vernal equinox, lay in Aries in Greek times but has since moved into Pisces».
