# Pez Austral

Un gran pez tumbado que bebe el agua que vierte Acuario, algo raro para un pez. Según Eratóstenes, es el padre de los dos
peces de Piscis, y salvó a la diosa siria Derceto cuando cayó a un lago; por eso, se decía, los sirios no comían pescado.
Como Piscis, su mito revela un origen babilonio. Fue la última constelación del catálogo de Ptolomeo.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Piscis Austrinus (ianridpath.com/startales/piscisaustrinus.html): «Eratosthenes called this the Great Fish and said that it was the parent of the two smaller fish of the zodiacal constellation Pisces. Like Pisces, its mythology has a Middle Eastern setting that reveals its Babylonian origin»; «the Syrian fertility goddess Derceto [...] is supposed to have fallen into a lake at Bambyce near the river Euphrates in northern Syria, and was saved by a large fish»; «as a result of this the Syrians do not eat fish»; «shown lying on its back and drinking water flowing from the urn of Aquarius»; «In the sky the fish is shown drinking the water flowing from the jar of Aquarius, a strange thing for a fish to do»; «The southern fish was the final constellation in Ptolemy's catalogue in the Almagest».
