# Hércules

Sorprende que el mayor héroe griego tenga una figura tan discreta: Eratóstenes identificó con Hércules a un personaje
arrodillado más antiguo, que pisa la cabeza del Dragón. Su cadera es un cuadrilátero de estrellas, la Piedra
Angular. En ella está M13, el mejor cúmulo globular del cielo del norte: Halley lo descubrió en 1714 y escribió que se ve a
simple vista con el cielo sereno y sin Luna.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Hercules (ianridpath.com/startales/hercules.html): «Heracles is the greatest of Greek and Roman heroes [...] So it is surprising that the Greeks allotted him a constellation only as an afterthought»; «the Greeks, who knew the figure as Ἐνγόνασι (Engonasi) [...] literally meaning 'the kneeling one'»; «Eratosthenes, a century after Aratus, identified the figure as Heracles»; «with one knee bent and a foot on the head of Draco, the dragon».
- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Hercules 2 (ianridpath.com/startales/hercules2.html): «Hercules is the fifth-largest constellation but is not particularly prominent»; «The four stars Epsilon, Zeta, Eta, and Pi Herculis form a distinctive quadrilateral known as the Keystone that outlines his pelvis»; «a globular cluster of stars, M13, the best example of such a cluster in northern skies. It was discovered by chance in 1714 by Edmond Halley [...] He described it as 'a little patch, but it shews it self to the naked eye when the sky is serene and the Moon absent'».
