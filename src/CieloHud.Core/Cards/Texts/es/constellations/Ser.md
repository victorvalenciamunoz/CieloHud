# Serpiente

La serpiente que sujeta Ofiuco, probablemente una culebra de Esculapio, que no es venenosa y se usaba en los templos de
curación griegos. Cuando se fijaron los límites oficiales de las constelaciones, a finales de los años veinte, se partió en
dos trozos, uno a cada lado de Ofiuco. En ella está la nebulosa del Águila, famosa por una foto del Hubble de sus pilares
de gas, donde nacen estrellas.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Serpens (ianridpath.com/startales/serpens.html): «Serpens represents a huge snake held by the constellation Ophiuchus»; «There is a species of snake known as the Aesculapian snake [...] These snakes are not venomous and were used in healing rituals in ancient Greece in temples called Asclepieia. This is presumably the type of snake that Ophiuchus/Asclepius is meant to be holding»; «When Eugène Delporte came to define the official constellation boundaries in the late 1920s [...] His solution [...] was to chop Serpens into the two halves that we see today, the head on one side of Ophiuchus and the tail on the other»; «M16, embedded in a gas cloud called the Eagle Nebula [...] The Eagle Nebula was the subject of a famous photograph by the Hubble Space Telescope showing pillars of gas and forming stars».
