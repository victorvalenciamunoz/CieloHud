# Telescopio

Uno de aquellos telescopios larguísimos colgados de un mástil, como los que usaba Cassini en el Observatorio de París: los
hacían tan largos para reducir los falsos colores de las lentes de la época. Lacaille lo dibujó subiendo entre Sagitario y
Escorpio, pero en el siglo XIX le recortaron el tubo y el mástil, y parte de sus estrellas pasaron a las vecinas.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Telescopium (ianridpath.com/startales/telescopium.html): «introduced by the Frenchman Nicolas Louis de Lacaille after his sky-mapping trip to the Cape of Good Hope in 1751–52»; «Telescopium represents the type of long, unwieldy refractor suspended from a pole known as an aerial telescope, as used by J. D. Cassini at Paris Observatory. The reason for the great length was to reduce chromatic aberration (false colour) produced by the crude lenses of that time»; «Lacaille originally depicted Telescopium as extending northwards between Sagittarius and Scorpius [...] but modern astronomers have cut off the top of the telescope's tube and mounting»; «Francis Baily was the first to crop it back in his British Association Catalogue of 1845 by allocating some of its stars to neighbouring constellations».
