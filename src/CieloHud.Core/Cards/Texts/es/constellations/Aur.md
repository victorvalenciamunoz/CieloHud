# Auriga

Un cochero sin carro que lleva en brazos una cabra y sus dos cabritos, algo que ningún mito explica. Suele identificarse
con Erictonio, rey de Atenas, el primero en enganchar cuatro caballos a un carro. La cabra, Capella, es Amaltea, que
según Arato amamantó a Zeus de niño. Según Eratóstenes, Zeus se hizo un manto con su piel: la égida, una palabra que
significa «piel de cabra».

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Auriga (ianridpath.com/startales/auriga.html): «With his right hand he grasps the reins of a chariot and a whip, while on his left arm he carries a goat and its two kids. Of his chariot itself there is no sign»; «the presence of the goat is not accounted for by any of them»; «The most popular interpretation is that he is Erichthonius, a legendary king of Athens»; «He became the first person to harness four horses to a chariot»; «Capella [...] According to Aratus it represented the goat Amaltheia, who suckled the infant Zeus»; «Eratosthenes says [...] he skinned the goat and made a cloak from its impenetrable hide [...] the so-called aegis of Zeus (the word aegis actually means 'goatskin')».
