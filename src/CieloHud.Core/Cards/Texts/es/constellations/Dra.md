# Dragón

Ladón, el dragón que guardaba el árbol de las manzanas de oro de Hera, y al que mató Hércules; en el cielo, Hércules le
pisa la cabeza. Se enrosca alrededor del polo norte del cielo, y hacia el 2800 a. C. una de sus estrellas, Thuban, hacía
de estrella polar, aún más cerca del polo que la actual. Los beduinos veían en las cuatro estrellas de su cabeza cuatro
camellas con su cría.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Draco (ianridpath.com/startales/draco.html): «Coiled around the sky's north pole is the celestial dragon»; «Legend has it that this is the dragon slain by Heracles during one of his labours, and in the sky the dragon is depicted with one foot of Heracles (in the form of the neighbouring constellation Hercules) planted firmly upon its head. This dragon, named Ladon, guarded the precious tree on which grew the golden apples»; «Hera had been given the golden apple tree as a wedding present»; «it was the north pole star around 2800 BC when it lay only 0.1° from the celestial pole, closer than our modern Polaris. Alpha Draconis is named Thuban»; «The stars Beta, Gamma, Nu, and Xi Draconis form a lozenge shape which we regard as the dragon's head [...] but which Bedouin Arabs visualized as four mother camels, al-'awā'idh, with a baby camel at the centre».
