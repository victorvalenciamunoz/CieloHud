# Mosca

Una mosca, junto al Camaleón: los navegantes holandeses Keyser y de Houtman habían visto en Madagascar a los camaleones
cazar moscas con la lengua. Johann Bayer, que no sabía qué insecto era, la llamó abeja, y ese nombre se usó durante dos
siglos. Lacaille la llamó mosca en 1756, y así se quedó.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Musca (ianridpath.com/startales/musca.html): «Musca was one of the 12 southern constellations introduced at the end of the 16th century by Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman»; «The constellation arose because the seafarers saw chameleons eating flies during their stopover on Madagascar, and in the sky the fly lies next to the constellation Chamaeleon»; «Johann Bayer, also in 1603, showed the insect [...] but called it Apis, the bee, an alternative title that was widely used for two centuries»; «Not knowing what it was meant to depict, Bayer wrongly identified it as a bee»; «The French astronomer Nicolas Louis de Lacaille correctly identified the insect as a fly (calling it la Mouche) on his southern planisphere of 1756».
