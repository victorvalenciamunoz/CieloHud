# Cruz del Sur

Para los griegos, sus estrellas eran las patas traseras de Centauro. La precesión, que mueve despacio el polo del cielo,
las ocultó a Europa, y los navegantes de los siglos XV y XVI las redescubrieron: la cruz les señalaba el polo sur del
cielo. En ella, recortada contra la Vía Láctea, hay una mancha oscura, el Saco de Carbón: una nube de gas y polvo que
describió Américo Vespucio en 1502.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Crux (ianridpath.com/startales/crux.html): «Its stars were known to the ancient Greeks [...] but at that time were regarded as part of the hind legs of Centaurus»; «They subsequently became lost from view to Europeans because of the effect of precession, which causes a gradual drift in the position of the celestial pole against the stars, and were rediscovered during the 15th and 16th centuries by seafarers venturing south»; «navigators were regularly using the cross as a pointer to the south celestial pole»; «Crux contains a famous dark cloud of gas and dust called the Coalsack Nebula, which appears in silhouette against the bright Milky Way background. This was first described by Amerigo Vespucci in 1502».
