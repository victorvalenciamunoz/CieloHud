# Lira

La primera lira, según el mito griego: Hermes la hizo con el caparazón de una tortuga y siete cuerdas, y Apolo se la dio
a Orfeo. Con su música, Orfeo convenció al dios del inframundo de que le devolviera a su esposa, Eurídice, con una
condición: no mirar atrás hasta salir a la luz. Miró, y la perdió para siempre. En los mapas antiguos se dibujaba a menudo
un águila detrás de la lira.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Lyra (ianridpath.com/startales/lyra.html): «It was the first lyre ever made, having been invented by Hermes»; «Hermes fashioned the lyre from the shell of a tortoise»; «tied across it seven strings of cow gut»; «Apollo later gave the lyre to Orpheus»; «the sound of his music charmed even the cold heart of Hades, god of the Underworld, who finally agreed to let Eurydice accompany Orpheus back to the land of the living on one solemn condition: Orpheus must not at any stage look behind him until the couple were safely back in daylight»; «He turned around to confirm that Eurydice was still there – and at that moment she slipped back into the depths of the Underworld, out of his grasp for ever»; «On European star maps the constellation was often depicted as an eagle positioned behind a lyre».
