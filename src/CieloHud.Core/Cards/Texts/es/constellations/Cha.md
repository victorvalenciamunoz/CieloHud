# Camaleón

Un camaleón, el lagarto que cambia de color, que los navegantes holandeses Keyser y de Houtman vieron en Madagascar en
1595. Persigue a la Mosca, su vecina: en 1600, el cartógrafo Hondius lo dibujó sacando la lengua para cazarla. Al principio
tenía las patas hacia el polo sur del cielo; Halley le dio la vuelta en 1678, y desde entonces le da la espalda. No tiene
leyendas.

## Fuentes

- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Chamaeleon (ianridpath.com/startales/chamaeleon.html): «The celestial chameleon, named after the colour-changing lizard, is one of the constellations representing exotic animals introduced by the Dutch navigators Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman»; «Chameleons are particularly common in Madagascar, where the Dutch fleet stopped to rest and resupply in 1595»; «Chamaeleon lies near the south celestial pole, in close pursuit of Musca, the fly»; «On a globe of 1600, the Dutch cartographer Jodocus Hondius [...] rearranged the pair to depict the chameleon sticking out its tongue to catch the fly»; «Plancius, Hondius, and Bayer oriented the chameleon with its feet towards the south celestial pole, but Edmond Halley turned it round so that it had its back to the pole on his southern star chart of 1678»; «Chamaeleon has no legends associated with it».
