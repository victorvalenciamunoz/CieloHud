# Seginus

Su nombre era al principio el de la constelación entera, el Boyero, en griego; los árabes lo deformaron al traducirlo, y
se volvió a deformar al pasarlo al latín, hasta no parecerse en nada. Es una estrella que late: su brillo cambia un 5 %
cada unas 7 horas. Y la rodea una pequeña nube de polvo que nadie sabe explicar.

## Fuentes

- Jim Kaler, Stars, Seginus (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/seginus.html): «a star name that actually originally meant "Bootes" in Greek and that was mangled in translation by the Arabs (and then RE-mangled when put back to Latin). It no longer sounds anything like the original»; «it is also variable, of a class known as a "Delta Scuti" star ... Seginus varies by around 5 percent, pulsating over a period of about 7 hours»; «Oddly, it is surrounded by a small cloud of dust visible only through its infrared radiation. Why it should exist is a mystery».
