# Mirzam

Desde el hemisferio norte sale poco antes que Sirio, y su nombre parece querer decir que la «anuncia», aunque no se sabe
bien qué significa. Es la más brillante de un tipo de estrellas que laten con varios ritmos a la vez, de unas 6 horas, y
que por ella se llaman también variables Beta Canis Majoris. Si estuviera tan cerca como Sirio, brillaría 15 veces más que
Venus.

## Fuentes

- Jim Kaler, Stars, Mirzam (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/mirzam.html): «Mirzam's name, something of a mystery, suggests that the star is an "announcer" of brilliant Sirius, as Mirzam rises first, but no one seems to know the exact meaning of the term»; «they are extremely complex and have multiple periods, the three strongest about a quarter of a day»; «Mirzam is the brightest of the "Beta Cephei variables" (after the first discovered), the class also called "Beta Canis Majoris variables."»; «If Mirzam were at Sirius's distance of 8.6 light years it would shine in our sky almost 15 times more brightly than Venus at its brightest».
- Jim Kaler, Stars, Sirius (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/sirius.html): «it has two "announcer stars" that from the mid-northern hemisphere rise before it, Procyon and Mirzam».
