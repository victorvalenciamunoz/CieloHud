# Alfa del Altar

El Altar es la más austral de las constelaciones antiguas: entero solo se ve desde más al sur de los 30° de latitud norte, y
ninguna de sus estrellas tiene nombre propio. Alfa es casi tan brillante como Beta, y quizá a veces más: gira tan deprisa, a
300 km por segundo, que la rodea un disco de gas, y su brillo cambia.

## Fuentes

- Jim Kaler, Stars, Alpha Ara (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/alphaara.html): «Ara, the Altar, taken in its entirety, is the most southerly of them all. Its main portion ... is fully visible only south of about 30 degrees north latitude, and though some of its stars are reasonably bright, none is named»; «the third magnitude (2.95) Alpha star just barely loses the title of Ara's brightest to Beta Arae»; «Alpha Arae's disk is related to its huge rotation velocity of 300 kilometers per second»; «The star has the reputation of being "eruptive," meaning that it varies its brightness ... Perhaps at times it indeed is the brightest of its constellation».
