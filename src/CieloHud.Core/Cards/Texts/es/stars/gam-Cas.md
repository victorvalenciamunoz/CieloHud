# Gamma de Casiopea

La estrella del centro de la W de Casiopea, y de las más brillantes sin nombre propio occidental; en la antigua China la
llamaban Tsih, «el látigo». Gira tan deprisa, 200 veces más que el Sol, que lanza gas a un disco a su alrededor, y su
brillo cambia sin que se pueda prever: a veces supera a Schedar, la Alfa. Fue la primera estrella de su tipo descubierta,
en 1866.

## Fuentes

- Jim Kaler, Stars, Gamma Cassiopeiae (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/gammacas.html): «At the center of the "W" lies our star»; «among the brightest stars in the sky that carries no proper "western" name, though known as Tsih (the whip) in old China»; «a true rotation speed of almost 400 kilometers per second, 200 times that of the Sun»; «The rotation, high luminosity, and perhaps atmospheric oscillations in an unknown way drive mass from the star into a surrounding disk»; «It's unpredictably variable»; «in 1866 one of the founders of the study of stellar spectra, Father Angelo Secchi, discovered that the star radiated light in specific colors ... Gamma Cas thus has the distinction of being the first known "Be star"».
- Jim Kaler, Stars, Shedar (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/shedar.html): «The strange variable star Gamma Cassiopeia can vault itself well beyond Shedar's apparent brightness».
