# Naos

Su nombre griego significa «el barco»: era una estrella del Argo, la nave de Jasón, que con el tiempo se dividió en
tres constelaciones; ella quedó en la Popa. Es de las estrellas más calientes del cielo, a unos 42 000 grados. Y es una
fugitiva: salió despedida de un cúmulo hace unos 2,5 millones de años y viaja a casi 100 km por segundo.

## Fuentes

- Jim Kaler, Stars, Naos (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/naos.html): «in the great craft of the Argonauts, Argo, lies Naos, the Greek name meaning "the ship." Argo is so large that in the nineteenth century, astronomers took to breaking it into parts, Carina, the Keel ..., Vela, the Sails, and Puppis, the Stern. Naos ... fell into Puppis»; «one of the brightest O stars (O5) in the sky, class O being the rarest and hottest of all normally classified stars»; «Its high temperature of 42,000 Kelvin»; «a class O "runaway" star, one that has been ejected at high speed (nearly 100 km/s) from its place of origin»; «Analysis of Naos's motion shows it to have been ejected two and a half million years ago from a cluster called Trumpler 10».
- Jim Kaler, Stars, Tureis (stars.astro.illinois.edu/sow/tureis.html): «the great ship Argo, in which Jason sailed to find the golden fleece».
- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Puppis (ianridpath.com/startales/puppis.html): la división del Argo, «by Nicolas Louis de Lacaille in his catalogue of the southern stars published in 1756». Kaler dice siglo XIX y Ridpath 1756: el texto no da fecha.
