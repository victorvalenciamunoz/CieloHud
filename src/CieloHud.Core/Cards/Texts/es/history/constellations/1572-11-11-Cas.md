# Tycho Brahe ve la «estrella nueva» de Casiopea

Tycho Brahe ve en la W de Casiopea una supernova, una «estrella nueva» tan brillante como Júpiter. Al medirla, demuestra que está tan lejos como las demás estrellas: el cielo también cambia.

## Fuentes

- NASA Science, Stellar Survivor from 1572 A.D. Explosion Supports Supernova Theory (science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/stellar-survivor-from-1572-ad-explosion-supports-supernova-theory/): «On November 11, 1572, Tycho Brahe noticed a star in the constellation Cassiopeia that was as bright as the planet Jupiter»; «No such star had ever been observed at this location before».
- NASA, NASA's Fermi Shows That Tycho's Star Shines in Gamma Rays (nasa.gov/mission_pages/GLAST/news/tycho-star.html): «The supernova first appeared around Nov. 6, but poor weather kept it from Tycho until Nov. 11». Por eso el texto no dice que fuera el primero en verla, aunque Ridpath diga «who first spotted it».
- ESA, 11 November (esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/11_November): «On 11 November 1572, the Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe first observed a 'new star'»; «one of the few recorded supernovae in the Milky Way»; «From his precise measurements Brahe showed that it was not merely a nearby object, such as a comet, but was in fact at the great distance of the stars. Thus, he demonstrated that the stars were not unchangeable».
- Ian Ridpath, Star Tales, Cassiopeia (ianridpath.com/startales/cassiopeia.html): «The five brightest stars of Cassiopeia are arranged in a distinctive W-shape»; «In November 1572 the familiar W-shape of Cassiopeia was disturbed by the intrusion of a bright interloper». Fecha en el calendario juliano: 1572 es anterior al gregoriano, que empezó el 15 oct 1582. Se deduce; las fuentes no lo dicen.
